Four signal families

The post is polished.
The proof may not be.

No single clue proves deception. Look for clusters: a claim, a motive, and inconsistencies that survive basic verification.

01 / ACCESS

Access framed as ownership

A rented car, borrowed watch, showroom, hotel lobby, or staged set is captioned to imply a permanent asset.

02 / MONEY

Unverifiable financial proof

Cropped dashboards, editable browser screens, missing dates, or gains with no cost basis can mimic evidence without supporting the claim.

03 / URGENCY

Lifestyle used as a sales funnel

The flex becomes proof of authority, then pressure: limited spots, private signals, a paid community, or an unregistered investment offer.

04 / CONSISTENCY

Details that do not travel

Dealer plates, repeated outfits, shifting stories, recycled locations, and reverse-image matches weaken a claimed timeline.

Verification playbook

A five-minute pause before you trust—or accuse.

Step 01 · Claim

Turn the vibe into a sentence you can test.

“This person is rich” is an impression. “This person says they own this car” is a claim. Write the smallest, fairest version before looking for evidence.

The bright line

Suspicion is not proof.

“A rented object can support a fake ownership claim. It can also be a perfectly ordinary prop. Context decides.”

Use the toolkit to examine public claims and commercial risk—not to harass creators, dox individuals, infer protected traits, or turn taste into a character judgment.

Say what you mean

A small glossary.

Back to top ↑
Noun LARPer

A person performing an unsupported identity or status claim online. Use carefully: describe the evidence before labeling the person.

Better: “The post implies ownership, but the location matches a rental studio.”

Verb To LARP

To perform a role, credential, lifestyle, or level of access as if it were authentic.

Example: “The account appears to be LARPing as an investment firm.”

Adjective LARPy

Informal shorthand for content that feels staged or performative. A feeling is a prompt to investigate, not evidence.

Better: name the inconsistency instead of relying on the adjective.

Related Clout chasing

Seeking attention or association with status. It overlaps with wealth LARPing, but does not necessarily involve a false factual claim.

Key distinction: attention-seeking versus deceptive representation.
